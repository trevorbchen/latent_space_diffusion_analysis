\section{Setup and theory roadmap}
\label{sec:design}

\textbf{Question.}
\looseness -1
We study diffusion in a latent space of dimension $\dlat$ when the
data occupy an effective $\dint$-dimensional subset. The central
object is the gap between the time at which a score model learns
population structure, $\taugen$, and the time at which it begins to
fit individual training samples, $\taumem$. The working hypothesis
is that the excess width $\dlat-\dint$ is not inert: it creates a
sequence of low-variance directions that the model must traverse
before reaching sample-specific modes.

\textbf{Synthetic data choices.}
\looseness -1
We use Gaussian-mixture data because it gives us the cleanest
control over the mechanism. A $k$-component mixture is sampled in a
signal subspace of dimension $\dint$, embedded into an ambient
latent space of dimension $\dlat$, and padded with isotropic
null-direction variance $\signoise^2$. The signal variance and
cluster scale control the population bulk; the null variance
controls the buffer bulk. This construction gives an analytic score
for the trainable MLP, exact knowledge of which coordinates are
signal versus null, and controlled sweeps in either $\dlat$ at
fixed $\dint$ or $\dint$ at fixed $\dlat$.

\textbf{RFNN theory.}
\looseness -1
For the random-feature network, freezing the first layer makes the
score-matching loss quadratic in the readout. Gradient flow then
diagonalizes in the eigenbasis of the empirical feature covariance
$\Umat$, and each mode is learned on timescale $1/\lambda_i$. The
theory section below derives the qualitative prediction we need:
anisotropic data split the usual population/sample spectrum into a
signal bulk, a noise-dimension buffer bulk, a sample bulk, and a
rank-null tail. A closed-form theorem for the bulk edges is still a
TODO; the current paper uses the RFNN eigenspectra as the
mechanistic microscope.

\textbf{Trainable MLP and real latents.}
\looseness -1
We then ask whether the same picture survives after replacing the
frozen RFNN with a trainable MLP score model. In synthetic data, the
hidden width is held fixed across the $\dlat$ sweep so parameter
count and per-step FLOPs stay roughly constant. In real data, we
train VAEs on CelebA and CIFAR-10, encode a diverse 1k-image subset,
and train the same latent-space MLP diffusion model for 5M steps
over five seeds. We measure memorization with the
\citet{somepalli2023diffusion} nearest-neighbor ratio test and
quality with FID.
