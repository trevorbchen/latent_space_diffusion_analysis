# Citation audit — revised nine-page manuscript

**Historical audit:** the subsequent corrections and remaining limitations are documented in [theory_repair.md](theory_repair.md). Line numbers below refer to the pre-correction source.

Audit date: 2026-09-25. Entry point: `main_iclr2027_9page.tex` in this directory, including its appendices.

**All 40 distinct cited works are real. Not all of the manuscript’s claims are supported by those works.** The main problems are incorrect scope, theorem attribution, and conflation of different operators or metrics—not invented references.

Scope: 132 citation-key occurrences, 40 cited works, 61 bibliography entries. All cited keys resolve. The 21 unused bibliography entries are outside this audit. The accompanying JSON preserves every inventoried citation context. Identity/publication checks used primary proceedings, publisher pages, author papers and arXiv; claim checks used the relevant text and theorem assumptions. “Supported” means an appropriate citation for the stated role, not independent validation of every manuscript proof. “Qualified” means relevant with scope limitations; “Mismatch” means at least one use needs correction. Status is per work, so a flagged work can have other entirely appropriate uses.

## Findings that matter most

1. **The central spectral specialization needs mathematical reconciliation.** In `sec9-theory.tex`, `sec9-intro.tex`, and `sec-appendix-theory.tex` (especially the four-block section and line 387), the manuscript presents its spectral decomposition as a direct specialization of Bonnaire. Their Theorem 3.2/C.2, in its specified overparameterized regime, gives a noise-averaged floor weight **1 − (n+d)/p**, at a nonzero floor location. The single-copy rank count **p−n** is a different statement for a different operator. Their covariance input is the clean-data covariance: substituting already-diffused eigenvalues requires an explicit mapping to avoid adding diffusion twice. A limiting spectral law also does not automatically determine fixed-rank outliers. Their general-covariance derivation uses replica reasoning. Reconcile these points before describing all four blocks as inherited from an existing theorem. [Source, Theorems 3.1–3.2 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17638v2).

2. **George’s pencil is described incorrectly.** The subspace comparison itself is appropriate, but published Appendix B explicitly constructs a **5×5 block pencil**, with sizes **(p, d−D, D, n, D)**. The manuscript lists only four sizes. Appendix C uses a six-block construction for another setting. Do not infer four spectral bands from the number of pencil blocks. [Published paper and PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/george26a.html).

3. **Training time and sampling time are conflated.** The opening paragraph groups Biroli with training-memorization studies; Biroli concerns collapse along the reverse generation trajectory. Its specific nearest-neighbor condensation threshold in the manuscript also needs to be identified as a manuscript derivation rather than a quoted result. Yi does not establish inevitable late-training memorization either. Replace the universal “generalize only for a while” claim with a conditional statement about settings in which continued training causes memorization. [Biroli](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54281-3), [Yi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14712).

4. **The GMM/Gaussian-equivalence citations are being asked to prove too much.** At `sec-appendix-theory.tex:253`, Liao–Couillet is said to treat O(1) same-cluster overlaps. Its normalized GMM assumptions instead have bounded means before division by √dimension and vanishing pairwise overlaps. The claimed contrast with fixed-norm centers needs reworking. More broadly, learning-risk equivalence, limiting empirical spectral distributions, edge convergence, and blockwise operator-norm equivalence are different results. Verify each needed conclusion against the assumptions of Hu–Lu, Goldt, Fan–Wang, Louart, and Benigni–Péché. [Liao–Couillet, Assumption 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/liao18a/liao18a.pdf).

5. **The nearest-neighbor metric is misattributed in the appendix.** At `sec-appendix-theory.tex:179` and `sec-appendix-theory.tex:825` (and related metric discussion), Somepalli is used for distance-ratio definitions. Its paper uses learned-feature replication retrieval; I could not locate these exact ratios or the 1/3 threshold there. Bonnaire Eq. 6 supports the generated-point-to-first/second-neighbor ratio. The alternative ratio whose denominator is the distance between training neighbors is a distinct metric and must not silently be substituted. Cite Somepalli for replication background, and document the actual implemented metric separately. [Somepalli](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.03860v3).

6. **The Tweedie step is not exact as written.** At `sec-appendix-theory.tex:876`, using a learned score defines a plug-in denoiser, not automatically the exact posterior mean. Moreover, the preceding approximation includes a remainder r. With the manuscript’s ζ = s_T − s_emp, substitution gives x̂ = x^μ + e^t Δ_t(ζ+r), not the displayed exact equality with ζ alone. Retain the remainder or state an approximation and bound its effect. [Efron](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3325056/).

7. **The cited Gaussian singular-value corollary does not match the bound.** At `sec-appendix-theory.tex:1114` and `:1139`, Corollary 7.3.3 of the first edition bounds the largest singular value, with an unspecified absolute concentration constant. It is not the quoted simultaneous sharp lower/upper bound. The lower-edge formula near line 1100 also needs a nonnegative-part qualification before squaring; an unrestricted negative lower bound cannot be squared. Check the appropriate sharp Gaussian theorem and dimensions, rather than changing only the citation number. [First-edition book](https://www.math.uci.edu/~rvershyn/papers/HDP-book/HDP-1.pdf). A candidate additional source to inspect is [Vershynin’s non-asymptotic survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3027).

8. **Other claims need narrower wording.** Near `sec-appendix-theory.tex:1156`, zero encoder means alone do not prove posterior collapse: posterior variance and informativeness matter ([Lucas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02469)). Near line 1376, multiplying separate MP lower edges is an approximation/bound under additional structure, not generally the exact free-product edge justified by El Karoui ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0492)). The appendix already acknowledges an approximate-edge limitation elsewhere; make the statements consistent. Local intrinsic-dimension papers also do not prove a universal latent-dimension inequality, and one-step regression feature-learning theory is an analogy for deeper diffusion networks.

The PDF was not rebuilt; the existing PDF and its nine-page main-text layout therefore remain unchanged, and do not yet incorporate the metadata edits. The substantive findings above remain open. This is a citation audit, not a complete proof audit or submission-readiness certification.
