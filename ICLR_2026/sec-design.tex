\section{Synthetic data and experimental setup}
\label{sec:design}

\textbf{Question and notation.}
\looseness -1
Diffusion models act on a latent space of dimension $\dlat$ even when the data occupy a much lower effective dimension $\dint$. In the synthetic theory, the key quantities are the time $\taugen$ when the score model learns population structure and the later time $\taumem$ when it begins to fit individual training samples. In the trainable and real-data experiments, we report the same transition more directly as the fraction of generated samples counted as memorized over training. The working hypothesis, made precise in Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}, is that the excess width $\dlat-\dint$ lowers the feature pre-activation variance $q$, which separates the slowest population mode from the fastest sample-specific mode by a factor that grows with $\dlat$; modes are learned in parallel, so it is this spectral ratio, not the number of excess directions, that delays memorization.

\textbf{Synthetic data choices.}
\looseness -1
We use Gaussian-mixture data because it gives us the cleanest control over the data. A $k$-component mixture is sampled in a signal subspace of dimension $\dint$, embedded into an ambient latent space of dimension $\dlat$ by some transformation, and padded with isotropic null-direction variance $\signoise^2$. The signal variance and cluster scale set the $\dint$ signal eigenvalues (a finite-rank spike set); the null variance $\signoise^2$ sets the noise-dimension block, which merges with the sample block on the diffusion floor $\Delta_t$ as $\signoise\to0$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-fourbulk}). This construction gives an analytic score for the trainable MLP, exact knowledge of which coordinates are signal versus null, and controlled sweeps in either $\dlat$ at fixed $\dint$ or $\dint$ at fixed $\dlat$.

\textbf{Model and evaluation controls.}
\looseness -1
We use the RFNN as a spectral microscope and the trainable MLP as a direct score-model test. In synthetic data, the hidden width is held fixed across the $\dlat$ sweep so parameter count and per-step FLOPs stay roughly constant. In real data, we train a VAE for each latent dimension on the full image training split, encode the same diverse 1k-image subset, and train a fixed-width latent-space MLP diffusion model for 5M steps over five seeds. We measure memorization with the \citet{somepalli2023diffusion} nearest-neighbor ratio test against the diffusion training subset. Image quality is measured with FID against the same subset. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} gives the full experimental details: synthetic data generation, RFNN spectrum estimation, trainable MLP training, VAE checkpointing, diverse-subset selection, latent diffusion training, and the memorization/FID evaluation schedule. Appendix~\ref{app:spatial-dit} also reports an exploratory spatial-DiT pilot on CelebA, which tests the same question in a more image-native architecture but introduces a spatial latent grid and extra tuning variables absent from the fixed-vector MLP runs.
