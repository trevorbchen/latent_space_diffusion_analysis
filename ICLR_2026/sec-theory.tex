\section{Theory: spectral buffer mechanism}
\label{sec:theory}

This section states what we can prove, sketch, or only conjecture about why excess latent dimensions delay memorization. The object is the frozen-feature score model of \cref{sec:rfnn} trained by gradient flow at a fixed diffusion time; every timescale is an eigenvalue of one matrix, $\Umat$, and the mechanism is a statement about the \emph{ratio} of two of its spectral edges. Each formal statement is tagged \emph{proved}, \emph{sketched} (a derivation with an explicitly named unproved step), or \emph{conjectured}, and is followed by one sentence on what it buys. Proofs are in \cref{app:theory-setup,app:theory-fourbulk,app:theory-buffer,app:theory-linear,app:theory-bridge,app:theory-predictor,app:theory-general,app:theory-scope}.

\subsection{Setup, standing assumptions, and the two timescales}
\label{sec:theory-setup}

\paragraph{Model and expected gradient flow.}
Fix $t>0$, $\Delta_t=1-e^{-2t}$, $x_t=e^{-t}x+\sqrt{\Delta_t}\,\eta$. The score is $s_A(x)=A\phi(x)$ with $\phi(x)=\pwidth^{-1/2}\tanh(Wx/\sqrt{\dlat})$, $W_{ij}\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ frozen, and $A\in\R^{\dlat\times\pwidth}$ trained from $A(0)=0$ by full-batch gradient descent on $\|\sqrt{\Delta_t}\,s_A(x_t^\mu)+\eta^\mu\|^2$ with a \emph{fresh} noise draw at every step. Its mean trajectory is exactly the Euler scheme of the gradient flow of $L_{\nsamp}(A)=\tfrac1{2\nsamp}\sum_\mu\E_\eta\|A\phi(x_t^\mu)-y^\mu\|^2$, $y^\mu=-\eta^\mu/\sqrt{\Delta_t}$ (\cref{prop:sgdmean-p1}, proved). With $\Umat=\tfrac1{\nsamp}\sum_\mu\E_\eta[\phi(x^\mu_t)\phi(x^\mu_t)^{\top}]=\sum_{i\le\pwidth}\lambda_iv_iv_i^{\top}$ and $V=\tfrac1{\nsamp}\sum_\mu\E_\eta[y^\mu\phi(x^\mu_t)^{\top}]$, the flow $\dot A=-(A\Umat-V)$ is diagonal in the eigenbasis of $\Umat$ (\cref{lem:gf-p1}, proved):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-decay-main}
a_i(T):=A(T)v_i=a_i^\star\bigl(1-e^{-\lambda_iT}\bigr),\qquad a_i^\star=Vv_i/\lambda_i ,
\end{equation}
so mode $i$ is absorbed on the clock $1/\lambda_i$. Two feature operators must be kept apart: the single-draw $\Umat^{(1)}=\Phi^{\top}\Phi/\nsamp$ has $\operatorname{rank}\le\nsamp$ (the machine-precision cliff at index $\nsamp$), whereas the $\E_\eta$ operator that governs training is generically full rank, its $\pwidth-\nsamp$ tail modes sitting contiguously below the sample block. The repository's learning-rate rule gives $T=0.02\,\dlat\times(\text{steps})$: \emph{ratios} of timescales are clock-free, step counts carry a factor $\dlat^{-1}$, and the ``spectral times'' quoted below are flow-time reciprocals, not steps (\cref{rem:clock-p1}).

\paragraph{Standing assumptions (\cref{ass:standing-p1}).}
(A1) fixed $t$; (A2) $\pwidth\ge\nsamp$ (violated at $\dlat=5$ in the $\pwidth=64\dlat$ sweep, where the tail block is empty); (A3) the clustered data of \cref{sec:design}, $x^\mu=Q(m_{c(\mu)}+\xi^\mu)$ with $k$ centres of norm $s$ in a $\dint$-dimensional subspace and $\xi^\mu\sim\mathcal N(0,\diag(\sigsig^2I_{\dint},\signoise^2I_{\dlat-\dint}))$, with $(k,s,\dint,\sigsig,\signoise,\nsamp)$ fixed and $\dlat\to\infty$ at fixed $\psi_p=\pwidth/\dlat$, so that $\psi_n=\nsamp/\dlat$ is \emph{not} fixed, must exceed $1$, and every population/sample separation degrades as $\psi_n\downarrow1$; because $\dint$ is fixed the signal block is a finite-rank spike set, not a bulk; (A4) with $\alpha_t^2=e^{-2t}(s^2/\dint+\sigsig^2)+\Delta_t$ and $\beta_t^2=e^{-2t}\signoise^2+\Delta_t$ the population signal and null eigenvalues of $M_t=\tfrac1\nsamp\sum_\mu\E[x_t^\mu x_t^{\mu\top}]$, the operating point is the pre-activation variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourbulk-q-main}
q(\dlat)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}M_t}{\dlat}=\beta_t^2+\frac{\dint(\alpha_t^2-\beta_t^2)}{\dlat},
\end{equation}
which decreases to $\beta_t^2$ (\cref{lem:q-p1}, proved), and the Gaussian-equivalence coefficients $a_1(q)=\E[\tanh'(\sqrt qz)]$, $a_*(q)^2=\E[\tanh(\sqrt qz)^2]-a_1(q)^2q$ \citep{pennington2017nonlinear,benigni2021eigenvalue,goldt2022gaussian,hu2022universality} are always evaluated at $q(\dlat)$: at $t=0.01$, $\signoise=0.5$, $q=2.76\to0.33$, $a_1^2=0.18\to0.63$ and $a_*^2=0.079\to0.0047$ over $\dlat=5\to200$, so nothing below is $\dlat$-independent; (A5) the sorted spectrum splits by index into $\mathcal S=[1,\dint]$, $\mathcal N=(\dint,\dlat]$, $\mathcal M=(\dlat,\nsamp]$, $\mathcal Z=(\nsamp,\pwidth]$ with $\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}>\lambda^{\mathcal N}_{\max}$ and $\lambda^{\mathcal N}_{\min}>\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}$ (measured $7.7$--$12\times$), no gap being assumed between $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal Z$; (A6$'$) a mass-weighted spectral-alignment hypothesis, used only for the generalisation gap.

\paragraph{Residual spectral error.}
Let $\pi_i=\|A^\star v_i\|^2$ and, for a block $\mathcal B$, $E_{\mathcal B}(T)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}\pi_ie^{-2\lambda_iT}$, $\Pi_{\mathcal B}=E_{\mathcal B}(0)$. Exactly, $\|A(T)-A^\star\|_F^2=E_{[1,\pwidth]}(T)$ and $L_{\nsamp}(A(T))-L_{\nsamp}(A^\star)=\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i\pi_ie^{-2\lambda_iT}$ (\cref{prop:rse-p1}, proved). Since $\Pi_{\mathcal Z}\gg\Pi_{\mathcal M}\gg\Pi_{\mathcal N}\gg\Pi_{\mathcal S}$ (ratios $10$--$10^3$ at $\dlat=20$), only block-restricted or loss-weighted functionals are observable.

\begin{definition}[Generalisation time; \cref{def:taugen-p1}]\label{def:taugen-main}
$\taugen(\varepsilon):=\inf\{T:E_{\mathcal S}(T)\le\varepsilon\,\Pi_{\mathcal S}\}$; $\tau_{\rm pop}(\varepsilon)$ is the same with $\mathcal S\cup\mathcal N$. In the isotropic case only $\tau_{\rm pop}$ is defined.
\end{definition}
$\taugen$ is the time at which the score on the signal subspace, the part that determines sample quality, is absorbed; the noise-dimension block (target $-x_{\mathcal N}/\beta_t^2$, exactly linear) costs time and buys no quality.

\begin{proposition}[$\taugen$ is set by the slowest signal mode; \cref{prop:taugen-p1}, proved]\label{prop:taugen-main}
With $i_\circ$ the slowest signal mode and $\theta_{\mathcal S}=\log(\Pi_{\mathcal S}/\pi_{i_\circ})$,
\begin{gather}
\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)-\theta_{\mathcal S}}{2\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}}\ \le\ \taugen(\varepsilon)\ \le\ \frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{2\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}},\label{eq:taugen-bounds-main}\\
\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}=\frac{a_1(q(\dlat))^2\,\bigl(e^{-2t}\ell_{\min}+\Delta_t\bigr)}{\dlat}\,(1+o(1)),\label{eq:lambdaSmin-main}
\end{gather}
where $\ell_{\min}$ is the smallest eigenvalue of the signal-subspace second moment of the realised training set (\cref{sec:theory-fourbulk}; the $(1+o(1))$ is the sketched step). Hence $\taugen$ \emph{grows} with $\dlat$ in flow time ($\propto\dlat/a_1(q(\dlat))^2$: $10.9\times$ over $\dlat=5\to200$ on the measured spectra) and \emph{falls} in repository steps ($158\to43$); it is $\dlat$-independent in neither clock.
\end{proposition}
This pins $\taugen$ to one eigenvalue whose $\dlat$-dependence is explicit, and it kills the earlier claim that $\taugen$ is flat in $\dlat$.

\begin{definition}[Memorisation times; \cref{def:taumem-p1,prop:gap-p1}]\label{def:taumem-main}
Let $G(T)$ be the test--train gap relative to initialisation and $\gamma_i=\langle a_i^\star,\bar Vv_i\rangle/(\lambda_i\pi_i)$ the population alignment of mode $i$ ($\bar V$ the population analogue of $V$). Exactly, $G$ is a quadratic form in $(1-e^{-\lambda_iT})_{i\le\pwidth}$, and under (A6$'$) $G=\sum_{i\in\mathcal M\cup\mathcal Z}g_i+o(1)$ with $g_i$ vanishing for population-aligned modes ($\gamma_i=1$). Split $\mathcal M=\mathcal M_{\rm spk}\cup\mathcal M_{\rm blk}$ by $\gamma_i>\gamma_0$: the spikes are cluster residuals whose absorption \emph{lowers} the test loss (the logged test loss still falls at $35$k--$90$k steps for $\dlat\ge40$, $20$--$60\times$ after the last population e-fold). Define the \emph{onset} $\taumem^{\rm onset}:=1/\lambda^{\rm blk}_{\max}$ and, for $\delta\in(0,1)$, $\taumem^{\rm gap}(\delta):=\inf\{T:G_{\rm blk}(T)\ge\delta G^{\rm blk}_\infty\}$; then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taumem-bounds-main}
\frac{\log\frac1{1-\delta}}{2\lambda^{\rm blk}_{\max}}\ \le\ \taumem^{\rm gap}(\delta)\ \le\ \frac{\log\frac1{1-\sqrt\delta}}{\lambda^{\rm blk}_{\min}} .
\end{equation}
The index-based $1/\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}$ used in \cref{sec:rfnn} is a lower bound on the onset, with equality when $\mathcal M_{\rm spk}=\emptyset$.
\end{definition}
The onset is the earliest time the gap can move; the bulk's slowest mode sets its completion. The paper's primary statistic is the memorised \emph{fraction} $F(T)$ \citep{somepalli2023diffusion,bonnaire2025}; a single sample edge would make any spectral surrogate of $F$ a step function, so $F$ needs the within-bulk decomposition of \cref{sec:theory-bridge,sec:theory-predictor}. The logged proxies (gen-gap $>0.02$; test loss within $5\%$ of its minimum) are absolute thresholds with a single-draw noise floor $\sqrt{2/(\nsamp\dlat)}$ and fire on the evaluation grid; the signal-block absorption deficit $D_{\mathcal S}(T)$ is the observable that tests \cref{def:taugen-main} (\cref{rem:proxy-p1}).

\subsection{Four spectral blocks of the feature correlation matrix}
\label{sec:theory-fourbulk}\label{sec:theory-edges}

Write $q_\mu=\|x_t^\mu\|^2/\dlat$ for the per-point pre-activation variance, $q=\tfrac1\nsamp\sum_\mu q_\mu\simeq\operatorname{tr}M_t/\dlat$ as in \eqref{eq:fourbulk-q-main}, $\sigma_r:=a_*(q)^2/\nsamp$, and split $M_t=e^{-2t}(\hat C+\hat S)+\Delta_tI$ into its $\dint\times\dint$ signal block $e^{-2t}(\hat C+\hat S_\parallel)+\Delta_tI$ and its null block $M_\perp=e^{-2t}\hat S_\perp+\Delta_tI$, where $\hat S_\perp$ is $\signoise^2$ times a white Wishart matrix of aspect ratio $\gamma_\perp=(\dlat-\dint)/\nsamp$. Set $\gamma_p=\nsamp/\pwidth$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{MP}}=\pwidth^{-1}(\sqrt{\nsamp-\dlat}+\sqrt{\pwidth-\dlat})^2$. Conditioning on $W$, the feature kernel is exactly a Hermite series, $K=A_1(X_tX_t^{\top}/\dlat)A_1+K_{\mathrm{res}}$ with $A_1=\diag(a_1(q_\mu))$ and $(K_{\mathrm{res}})_{\mu\mu}=a_*(q_\mu)^2$ (\cref{lem:fourbulk-kernel}, proved); the $\tanh$ features are centred, so no rank-one term appears.

\begin{proposition}[Four spectral blocks of $\Umat$; sketched]\label{prop:fourbulk-main}
Assume $\pwidth\ge\nsamp>\dlat>\dint$, $\dint$ and $t>0$ fixed, and $\dlat,\nsamp,\pwidth\to\infty$ with $\gamma_\perp,\gamma_p$ fixed. Under Gaussian equivalence of the feature Gram (\cref{ass:fourbulk-ge}: proved for the bulk by \citealp{louart2018random,liao2018spectrum}; open for the $\dint$ outliers and for the $\xi$-average), the ordered spectrum of $\Umat$ consists of four index blocks:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item[(i)] $\dint$ \emph{signal} outliers, $\lambda^{\mathrm{sig}}_j=\tfrac{a_1(q)^2}{\dlat}\bigl[e^{-2t}\lambda_j(\hat C+\hat S_\parallel)+\Delta_t\bigr]\bigl(1+O(\pwidth^{-1/2})\bigr)+\sigma_r(1+O(\gamma_p))$, population mean $a_1(q)^2\alpha_t^2/\dlat$;
\item[(ii)] $\dlat-\dint$ \emph{noise-dimension} eigenvalues, those of $\tfrac{a_1(q)^2}{\dlat}M_\perp^{1/2}(W_\perp^{\top}W_\perp/\pwidth)M_\perp^{1/2}$: mean $a_1(q)^2\beta_t^2/\dlat$ exactly, support $\tfrac{a_1(q)^2}{\dlat}\bigl[e^{-2t}\signoise^2(1\pm\sqrt{\gamma_\perp})^2+\Delta_t\bigr]$ at leading order in $\dlat/\pwidth$, edges corrected by $O(\sqrt{\dlat/\pwidth})$, median $\pwidth$-independent;
\item[(iii)] $\nsamp-\dlat$ \emph{sample} eigenvalues: the Marchenko--Pastur--Silverstein image, with $\pwidth-\dlat$ effective samples, of the residual kernel $P_\perp K_{\mathrm{res}}P_\perp/\nsamp$; mean $\sigma_r(1-\dlat/\pwidth)\,r_t(q)$ and top edge $\kappa_{\mathrm{top}}\sigma_r$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{top}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{MP}}$ for a homogeneous residual and a single copy, and otherwise set by the spread of $a_*(q_\mu)^2$ and the same-cluster part of $K_{\mathrm{res}}$;
\item[(iv)] $\pwidth-\nsamp$ \emph{residual-floor} eigenvalues of total mass $\simeq(1-r_t(q))\,a_*(q)^2$, exact zeros for a single diffused copy (\cref{rem:fourbulk-singlecopy}).
\end{itemize}
Here $r_t(q)=\kappa_{\rm res}(q-\Delta_t)/\kappa_{\rm res}(q)\in(0,1]$ is the fraction of the nonlinear feature mass coherent across noise draws of one point ($\kappa_{\rm res}$ the residual kernel; $0.97\to0.82$ over the sweep at $t=0.01$). The block means satisfy the exact trace identity
\begin{align}
&\dint\tfrac{a_1^2\alpha_t^2}{\dlat}+(\dlat-\dint)\tfrac{a_1^2\beta_t^2}{\dlat}+\nsamp\,\sigma_r\nonumber\\
&\qquad=a_1(q)^2q+a_*(q)^2=\E\bigl[\tanh(\sqrt qz)^2\bigr],\label{eq:fourbulk-trace-main}
\end{align}
in which the data-side terms sum to $a_1(q)^2q$, the residual mass $\nsamp\sigma_r=a_*(q)^2$ splits exactly into $\dlat\sigma_r(1+\tfrac{\nsamp-\dlat}{\pwidth})$ of outlier shifts plus $(\nsamp-\dlat)\sigma_r(1-\tfrac{\dlat}{\pwidth})$ in the sample bulk, and the right side is $O(1)$ for every $\dlat$,
while $\operatorname{tr}\Umat=\tfrac1\nsamp\sum_\mu\E\tanh(\sqrt{q_\mu}z)^2$ exactly (Jensen correction $2$--$5\%$; measured $\operatorname{tr}\Umat_{\rm code}/\pwidth$ matches to $1.5\%$ at every $\dlat$).
\end{proposition}
Every data-side eigenvalue scales as $a_1(q(\dlat))^2/\dlat$ and is $\pwidth$-independent at leading order in $\dlat/\pwidth$ (the code normalisation $\Umat_{\rm code}=\pwidth\Umat$ reintroduces $\psi_p$); the sample block scales as $a_*(q(\dlat))^2/\nsamp$. An edge law that divided every block by $\psi_p$ would make the left side of \eqref{eq:fourbulk-trace-main} $O(\dlat/\pwidth)$ and fail the check by a factor $64$ at $\dlat=40$. The counts follow from rank alone: the $\nsamp\times\nsamp$ Gram equals a rank-$\dlat$ data term plus the residual kernel, so interlacing leaves $\nsamp-\dlat$ eigenvalues inside the residual bulk and $\dlat$ above it once the data directions are outliers (\cref{lem:fourbulk-counts,lem:fourbulk-bbp}, proved); the last non-residual index is $\nsamp$, not $\dlat+\nsamp$. What the proposition buys: parameter-free block positions whose drift with $\dlat$ (through $q$ alone) is reproduced in \cref{tab:fourbulk-numbers-main}, whereas a fixed-$a_1$ theory predicts flat blocks ($64.9$ and $6.22$ in code units) and is rejected.

\begin{corollary}[Separation conditions; sketched]\label{cor:fourbulk-separation-main}
Under \cref{prop:fourbulk-main}, at leading order in $\dlat/\pwidth$ the blocks are disjoint iff
\begin{gather}
\lambda_{\min}(\hat C+\hat S_\parallel)\;>\;\signoise^2\bigl(1+\sqrt{\gamma_\perp}\bigr)^2,\label{eq:fourbulk-sep1-main}\\
\tfrac{\nsamp}{\dlat}\,\tfrac{a_1(q)^2}{a_*(q)^2}\Bigl[e^{-2t}\signoise^2\bigl(1-\sqrt{\gamma_\perp}\bigr)^2+\Delta_t\Bigr]\;>\;\kappa_{\mathrm{top}} ,\label{eq:fourbulk-sep2-main}
\end{gather}
(signal $\succ$ noise-dim and noise-dim $\succ$ sample, respectively). In \eqref{eq:fourbulk-sep1-main} $a_1^2$, $1/\dlat$ and $\Delta_t$ cancel exactly; with population means it reads $(s^2/\dint+\sigsig^2)/\signoise^2>(1+\sqrt{\gamma_\perp})^2$ ($11.2$ vs $\le2.7$ at $\signoise=0.5$), and $\pwidth$ never enters. In \eqref{eq:fourbulk-sep2-main} the left side carries $\nsamp/\dlat$, the de-saturation ratio $a_1^2/a_*^2$ (which grows $2.3\to134$ over the sweep) and a bracket that closes as $\dlat\to\nsamp$ or $\signoise\to0$; the measured gap $\lambda^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\min}/\lambda^{\mathrm{samp}}_{\max}$ is non-monotone ($4.4,7.7,13,18,12$ at $\dlat=10,\dots,200$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Isotropic and $\signoise\to0$ limits]\label{rem:fourbulk-isotropic-main}
For $\signoise=\sigsig$ condition \eqref{eq:fourbulk-sep1-main} fails as soon as $\gamma_\perp\gtrsim0.1$ and blocks (i)--(ii) merge into a single population bulk with $\dint$ BBP-type outliers at its top: this is $\rho_2$ of \citet{bonnaire2025}, and (iii) is their $\rho_1$. The $\dlat-\dint$ noise-dimension modes therefore \emph{exist} in the isotropic case and remain separated from the sample bulk by \eqref{eq:fourbulk-sep2-main} (at $\dlat=100$: lower edge $7.4$ predicted, $7.4$--$7.5$ measured, $12\times$ above the sample top); anisotropy adds the split of $\rho_2$ into (i) and (ii), not the existence of (ii). Conversely, as $\signoise\to0$ the noise-dimension eigenvalues collapse onto the diffusion floor $a_1^2\Delta_t/\dlat$, which is comparable to $\kappa_{\rm top}\sigma_r$, and blocks (ii)--(iii) merge (\cref{rem:fourbulk-sigperp0}): the smooth data/sample transition seen at $\signoise=0.01$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Relation to prior spectral results.}
Theorem~3.1 of \citet{bonnaire2025} gives the limiting density of $\Umat$ for an \emph{arbitrary} population spectral measure $\rho_\Sigma$; \cref{prop:fourbulk-main} is that theorem evaluated at the two-atom measure $\rho_\Sigma=\tfrac{\dint}{\dlat}\delta_{\alpha_t^2}+(1-\tfrac{\dint}{\dlat})\delta_{\beta_t^2}$, with the caveat that at fixed $\dint$ the signal atom has vanishing mass and block (i) is governed by spiked-covariance perturbation theory \citep{baik2005phase} rather than by a bulk. The bulk half of the Gaussian-equivalence hypothesis is a theorem for deterministic data \citep{louart2018random,liao2018spectrum}. \citet{george2025dsm} already analyse a linear pencil with block dimensions $(\pwidth,\dlat-\dint,\dint,\nsamp)$ for data supported exactly on a $\dint$-dimensional subspace ($\signoise=0$), which is the merged limit of \cref{rem:fourbulk-sigperp0}. We do not extend either result; what is new is strictly positive $\signoise$, which lifts (ii) off the sample bulk by $e^{-2t}\signoise^2$ and gives it a Marchenko--Pastur width of ratio $\gamma_\perp$, the reading of the blocks as gradient-flow learning-rate bands, and everything downstream (\cref{sec:theory-buffer,sec:theory-bridge,sec:theory-predictor}).

\subsection{The buffer is a ratio of two spectral edges}
\label{sec:theory-buffer}

Absorbing mode $i$ to tolerance $\varepsilon$ takes $\tau_i(\varepsilon)=\lambda_i^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)$ by \eqref{eq:mode-decay-main}. For the edge statistics we use $\taugen=\log(1/\varepsilon)/\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}$ (the upper bound in \eqref{eq:taugen-bounds-main} up to the factor $2$) and the onset $\taumem^{\rm on}=\log(1/\varepsilon)/\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}$ (\cref{def:abs-times-buf}); their ratio $R:=\taumem^{\rm on}/\taugen=\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}/\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}$ is free of $\varepsilon$, of the learning rate and of the code normalisation. Write $\kappa_{\rm top}(\dlat):=\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}\nsamp/a_*(q)^2$ for the sample-bulk top in units of its residual mass per point (the measured column of \cref{tab:fourbulk-numbers-main}; it equals $r_t\,g$ in the notation of \cref{app:theory-buffer}).

\begin{proposition}[$\taugen$ and $\taumem^{\rm on}$ along the sweep; \cref{prop:taugen-buf,prop:onset-buf}]\label{prop:buffer-main}
Under \cref{assump:inputs-buf} (Gaussian equivalence at block scale, coherent $\xi$-averaging, $\pwidth\ge\nsamp>\dlat$, the edges of \cref{prop:fourbulk-main}, and block separation, which holds at $\signoise=0.5$ but not at $\signoise=0.01$),
\begin{gather}
\taugen=\frac{\dlat\,\log(1/\varepsilon)}{a_1(q(\dlat))^2\,\bigl(e^{-2t}\ell_{\min}+\Delta_t\bigr)}\,(1+o(1)),\label{eq:tau-scalings-buf}\\
\taumem^{\rm on}=\frac{\nsamp\,\log(1/\varepsilon)}{a_*(q(\dlat))^2\,\kappa_{\rm top}(\dlat)}\,(1+o(1)),\label{eq:tauon-main}
\end{gather}
where $\ell_{\min}(\dlat)=\nu_{\min}\bigl(1+\gamma_\perp\signoise^2/(\nu_{\min}-\signoise^2)\bigr)$ is the smallest signal-subspace data eigenvalue $\nu_{\min}$ (law independent of $\dlat$ at fixed centres) lifted by the spiked-covariance factor of the null block \citep{baik2005phase} (proved given the edges). The prefactor $\dlat/a_1(q(\dlat))^2$ is strictly increasing (proved), so $\taugen$ is sandwiched between two linear functions of $\dlat$ with slope ratio $a_1(q_\infty)^2/a_1(q_{\max})^2=3.7$; $\kappa_{\rm top}$ is bracketed by interlacing between the $(\dlat{+}1)$-th and the first eigenvalue of an $\nsamp$-mode Wishart with population Gram $\bar K$, falls from $\approx16$--$19$ at $\dlat\le10$ to $\approx1.4$ at $\dlat=200$, and has no closed form at small $\dlat$, where the cubic residual features span only $\binom{\dlat+2}{3}<\nsamp$ dimensions and $\bar K$ is rank-deficient (sketched).
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[The buffer; \cref{cor:buffer-buf}, proved from \eqref{eq:tau-scalings-buf}]\label{cor:buffer-main}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-main}
R(\dlat)=\frac{\nsamp}{\dlat}\cdot\frac{a_1(q)^2}{a_*(q)^2}\cdot\frac{e^{-2t}\ell_{\min}+\Delta_t}{\kappa_{\rm top}},
\end{equation}
up to a factor $1+o(1)$, with $q=q(\dlat)$, $\ell_{\min}=\ell_{\min}(\dlat)$ and $\kappa_{\rm top}=\kappa_{\rm top}(\dlat)$. $\dlat$ enters through the fan-in $1/\dlat$ of the data-side edges, through $q(\dlat)$, and through $\kappa_{\rm top}$ and $\ell_{\min}$; it never multiplies an absorption time. $R$ opens with $\dlat$ exactly when $a_1^2/a_*^2$ grows faster than $\nsamp/\dlat$ shrinks.
\end{corollary}
At $\signoise=0.5$ the buffer opens over the whole released sweep because $\tanh$ de-saturates: $a_1^2/a_*^2$ grows $58\times$ and $\kappa_{\rm top}$ collapses $13\times$ while $\nsamp/\dlat$ shrinks $40\times$. Holding $q$ fixed as $\dlat$ grows removes the growth ($R$ changes $1.09\times$ against $3.17\times$ over $\dlat=40\to240$; \cref{rem:normfixed-buf}), so the mode count is not the driver. On the repository spectra ($\pwidth=64\dlat$, eigenvalues divided by $\pwidth$) $\taugen$ grows $11\times$ over $\dlat=5\to200$ (predicted $10.8\times$), $\taumem^{\rm on}$ grows $221\times$ ($16.7\times$ from $a_*^2$, $13\times$ from $\kappa_{\rm top}$), and $R$ grows monotonically, $21\to428$. Once $q\approx q_\infty=\beta_t^2$, i.e.\ beyond $d^\star=\dint(\alpha_t^2-\beta_t^2)/\beta_t^2=47$, \eqref{eq:R-main} predicts a slow decline like $\nsamp/\dlat$ tempered by $\ell_{\min}$ and $\kappa_{\rm top}$; this saturated phase is \emph{conjectural} and not yet observed (simulated $R$ is flat over $\dlat=200$--$480$; \cref{prop:phases-buf}). What the corollary buys is the correct \emph{form} of the buffer: a product of explicit functions of $q(\dlat)$ that a different $\signoise$ changes before any run.

\begin{remark}[A mode count cannot multiply a timescale]\label{rem:nonsequitur-main}
An earlier draft bounded $\taumem-\taugen$ below by $(\dlat-\dint)/\lambda^{\mathcal N}_{\min}$, as if the readout traversed the noise-dimension modes in sequence. Under the diagonal flow \eqref{eq:mode-decay-main} all modes decay in parallel and the absorption time of any sample mode is $\lambda_j^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)$ whatever the number or rates of the other modes; the asserted bound is violated at every $\dlat\ge20$ on the repository's own spectra. The intermediate block affects $R$ only through $q(\dlat)$, $\kappa_{\rm top}$ and $\ell_{\min}$ (\cref{rem:nonsequitur-buf}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Onset, not median]\label{rem:median-main}
The claim is about the \emph{onset} of sample-specific learning. At small $\dlat$ the residual Gram is rank-deficient and spiked, so most of the sample-block mass sits in a few top modes above a heavy lower tail ($\lambda^{\mathcal M}_{\max}/\mathrm{median}\approx540$ at $\dlat=5$, $\approx2$ at $\dlat=200$); as $\dlat$ grows the block becomes a proper Marchenko--Pastur bulk and its median rises toward the mean while $\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}\propto a_1^2/\dlat$ falls. Hence $\lambda^{\mathcal S}_{\min}/\mathrm{median}(\lambda^{\mathcal M})$ \emph{shrinks} $14\times$ over the sweep while $R$ grows $20\times$ (\cref{rem:median-buf}). The time to absorb the bulk of the sample modes, which governs the memorised fraction, is a different quantity (\cref{sec:theory-bridge}). Under isotropy ($\signoise=\sigsig$) de-saturation is exhausted by $d^\star=8.8$ and $R$ is non-monotone with a peak near $\dlat\approx40$ (simulated; \cref{rem:isotropic-buf}); the four-bulk \emph{structure} is absent there, not the noise-dimension modes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An exactly solvable linear score model}
\label{sec:theory-linear}

The part of the mechanism that needs no random-matrix input is the null block. Write $x_t=(x_{t,\mathsf S},x_{t,\mathsf N})$ in the rotated frame, $\gamma_t^2=e^{-2t}\sigsig^2+\Delta_t$, so the noised marginal is a Gaussian mixture with component covariance $\Sigma_t=\diag(\gamma_t^2I_{\dint},\beta_t^2I_{\dlat-\dint})$ and second moment $M_t=\diag(M_{t,\mathsf S},\beta_t^2I)$ (\cref{def:lin-setting}).

\begin{lemma}[Null block and best linear score; \cref{lem:null-exact-lin,prop:lin-gf-closed}, proved]\label{lem:null-exact-lin-main}
The null block of the true score is exactly linear, $s^\star_{\mathsf N}(x_t)=-x_{t,\mathsf N}/\beta_t^2$, because the cluster means have no null component, and $\E[s^\star x_t^{\top}]=-I$ (Stein). Hence the risk $L_t(B)=\E\|Bx_t-s^\star\|^2$ of a linear score $s_B(x)=Bx$ is a quadratic with unique minimiser $B^\star_t=-M_t^{-1}$, exact on $\mathsf N$ and the best linear approximation on $\mathsf S$ (irreducible residual $R_{\rm irr}=1.68$ at $t=0.01$ for the repository's centres). Gradient flow from $B_0=0$ gives $B(T)=-M_t^{-1}(I-e^{-2M_tT})$ and excess risk
\begin{align}
\mathcal E(T)&=\sum_{i=1}^{\dlat}\frac{e^{-4\lambda_i(M_t)T}}{\lambda_i(M_t)}
=\sum_{i\le\dint}\frac{e^{-4\lambda^{\mathsf S}_iT}}{\lambda^{\mathsf S}_i}\nonumber\\
&\qquad+(\dlat-\dint)\,\frac{e^{-4\beta_t^2T}}{\beta_t^2}.\label{eq:lin-excess-main}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
Each mode's initial risk is its target energy $1/\lambda_i$, so the slow null modes are also the largest and dominate the late residual; the per-dimension score error $\mathrm{err}=[\mathcal E+R_{\rm irr}]/\dlat$ obeys $\dlat\cdot\mathrm{err}=a(T)+(\dlat-\dint)\,b(T)$, affine in $\dlat$ with the single slope $b(T)=e^{-4\beta_t^2T}/\beta_t^2$ (\cref{cor:lin-blocks}). (The factor $2$ in the exponent is the loss convention without the $\tfrac12$ of $L_\nsamp$; ratios of clocks are unaffected.) Trained on the \emph{empirical} risk the same flow converges to $-\widehat M_t^{-1}$, whose null block is $e^{-2t}\signoise^2\mathcal W+\Delta_tI$ with $\mathcal W$ white Wishart: the null clock is a Marchenko--Pastur band $e^{-2t}\signoise^2(1\pm\sqrt{\gamma_\perp})^2+\Delta_t$ whose slow edge grows with $\dlat/\nsamp$, under-sampled directions overshoot the population score, and the late-time population error per dimension has a floor $F_t(\gamma_\perp)=\gamma_\perp(e^{-2t}\signoise^2)^2/\beta_t^6+O(\gamma_\perp^2)$ rising with $\dlat$ (\cref{prop:lin-emp}, proved). Substituting $M_t\to\Umat$ reproduces \eqref{eq:mode-decay-main} exactly and, under Gaussian equivalence, identifies the $\dlat$ data-side eigenvalues as $a_1(q)^2\widehat\lambda_i(\widehat M_t)/\dlat$, i.e.\ block (ii) of \cref{prop:fourbulk-main} with its MP width (\cref{cor:lin-rfnn}, sketched). What the lemma buys: a rigorous anchor for the noise-dimension clock and for the finite-$\nsamp$ overfitting of the null block, which is the linear-model form of the return of memorisation as $\dlat\to\nsamp$; what it cannot see is the sample bulk, since a linear model never reproduces training points (\cref{rem:lin-nomem}).

\subsection{From residual error to near-duplicate generation}
\label{sec:theory-bridge}\label{sec:theory-fraction}\label{sec:theory-mem}

The spectral clocks fix \emph{when} the sample bulk is absorbed; they say nothing about \emph{why} absorbing it produces near-duplicates. The bridge is the closed-form score of the noised empirical measure \citep{scarvelis2023closedform,gu2023memorization}, $s_{\rm emp}(x,t)=\sum_\mu w_\mu(x,t)(e^{-t}x^\mu-x)/\Delta_t$ with softmax weights $w_\mu\propto\exp(-\|x-e^{-t}x^\mu\|^2/2\Delta_t)$ (\cref{def:bridge-objects}). Write $\mathcal E_t(T)=\tfrac1\nsamp\sum_\mu\E\|s_T-s_{\rm emp}\|^2$ for the residual of the partially trained score on noised training points; for the RFNN it is $\sum_i\lambda_i\|a_i^\star\|^2e^{-2\lambda_iT}$ plus a rank-null floor, and for $T\gg\taugen$ it is carried by the sample bulk. Let $d_{\rm NN}(\mu)$ be the training-set nearest-neighbour distance and $G_{\rm NN}$ its empirical CDF. Two elementary facts (\cref{lem:condensation,lem:endpoint}, proved): the weights concentrate on one training point whenever $e^{2t}\Delta_t<d_{\rm NN}(\mu)^2/(2\log\nsamp)$ \citep{biroli2024dynamical}, and once they have, the exact empirical sampler stopped at $t_{\min}$ lands at $x^\mu+\sqrt{\Delta_{t_{\min}}}\,\eta$. So even a perfect memoriser is reported as memorised by the $1/3$ nearest-neighbour-ratio test \citep{somepalli2023diffusion} only if $3\sqrt{\dlat\Delta_{t_{\min}}}<d_{\rm NN}(\mu)$: the test has a \emph{metric ceiling} $1-G_{\rm NN}(3\sqrt{\dlat\Delta_{t_{\min}}})$, equal to $0.68$ at $\dlat=5$ and $\to1$ for $\dlat\ge25$ at $\signoise=0.5$ but $\le0.05$ for $\dlat\ge20$ at $\signoise=0.01$ (the released Euler--Maruyama sampler raises the terminal variance above $\Delta_{t_{\min}}$ by $\approx36\%$, which lowers every ceiling further).

\begin{proposition}[Bridge; \cref{prop:bridge}, sketched]\label{prop:bridge-main}
Fix an attachment time $t\in[t_{\min},t_c]$ and assume (B1) condensation at $t$; (B2) the residual $s_T-s_{\rm emp}$ at a noised training point is approximately isotropic Gaussian with total variance $\mathcal E_{t,\mu}(T)$, independent of the forward noise (justified when many sample modes are still unabsorbed and $\dlat\gg1$); (B3) the endpoint is the Tweedie denoiser of the learned score at $t$ plus the sampler's terminal noise. Then the generated point is $x^\mu+\delta$ with $\delta\sim\mathcal N(0,\Delta_{\rm eff,\mu}(T)I)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Deff-main}
\Delta_{\rm eff,\mu}(T)=\Delta_{t_{\min}}+\frac{e^{2t}\Delta_t^{2}}{\dlat}\,\mathcal E_{t,\mu}(T),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the law of the \emph{exact} empirical sampler stopped at noise level $\Delta_{\rm eff}$, and the reported fraction is
\begin{align}
F_{\rm mem}(T)&=\frac1\nsamp\sum_\mu\mathbf 1\!\Bigl[d_{\rm NN}(\mu)>3\sqrt{\dlat\,\Delta_{\rm eff,\mu}(T)}\Bigr]\label{eq:Fmem-main}\\
&\approx1-G_{\rm NN}\!\Bigl(3\sqrt{\dlat\Delta_{t_{\min}}+e^{2t}\Delta_t^{2}\,\mathcal E_t(T)}\Bigr).\nonumber
\end{align}
The metric criterion implies condensation at the effective level whenever $\dlat>\tfrac29e^{2t_c}\log\nsamp$ (worst case $10.6$ at $\dlat=200$), so it is the metric, not condensation, that binds.
\end{proposition}
Two consequences (\cref{cor:taumem-fraction,cor:dnn-scaling}, sketched). (a) A single sample-bulk edge would make $F_{\rm mem}$ a step in $T$; the measured smooth curves come from the spread of $d_{\rm NN}$ (data level: $d^{(0.9)}_{\rm NN}/d^{(0.1)}_{\rm NN}=2.3$ at $\dlat=5$, $1.1$ at $\dlat=200$) and from the within-bulk spread of $\lambda_i$ (model level). The level-$f$ time is $\taumem(f)=\inf\{T:\mathcal E_t(T)\le[(d^{(1-f)}_{\rm NN})^2-9\dlat\Delta_{t_{\min}}]/(9e^{2t}\Delta_t^2)\}$: the most isolated training points are memorised first, and for a single-exponential residual $\taumem(f_2)-\taumem(f_1)=\lambda_{\rm samp}^{-1}\log\bigl(d^{(1-f_1)}_{\rm NN}/d^{(1-f_2)}_{\rm NN}\bigr)$ up to a $\dlat\Delta_{t_{\min}}$ correction. (b) Approximately, $d_{\rm NN}^2\approx C\sigsig^2(\nsamp/k)^{-2/\dint}\kappa_\mu+2\signoise^2(\dlat-\dint)-O(\signoise^2\sqrt{(\dlat-\dint)\log\nsamp})$: when the null term is negligible ($\signoise=0.01$) the data-side threshold scales as $\nsamp^{-2/\dint}$ and is $\dlat$-independent, a $\dint$, not $\dlat$, exponent, so every $\dlat$-dependence of $\taumem$ then comes from the spectrum; when it dominates ($\signoise=0.5$, $\dlat\gtrsim20$) the criterion becomes the per-coordinate condition $\Delta_{\rm eff}\lesssim2\signoise^2/9$ and the same distance concentration pushes the NN-ratio of non-memorised samples toward $1$ (measured mean ratio $0.52\to0.87$). For VAE latents the test is applied after decoding; with bi-Lipschitz decoder constants $\ell\le L$ and coincident nearest neighbours in the two spaces, $F_{\rm lat}(\ell/3L)\le F_{\rm pix}(1/3)\le F_{\rm lat}(L/3\ell)$, and $L/\ell$ is uncontrolled across the VAE sweep (\cref{rem:pixel}, conjectured). What is \emph{not} proved is (B2): the true residual is a blend $(1-m)\hat x_{\rm pop}+m\,\hat x_{\rm emp}$ of the two denoisers, and a one-step evaluation of the blend gives an $O(1)$ absorption threshold $m_\star\approx0.5$ that agrees with \eqref{eq:Deff-main} on the ordering of $\taumem$ with $\dlat$ but not on its constant; \cref{app:theory-bridge} states the zero-parameter test (compute $\mathcal E_{t_{\min}}(T)$ against the closed form during a rerun) that decides between them.

\subsection{From the frozen spectrum to a fraction predictor}
\label{sec:theory-predictor}

The released predictor of \cref{sec:spectral-predictor} reads $P_d(s)=\mathrm{mean}_i(1-e^{-\kappa\lambda_i(M_t)s})^2$ off $\mathrm{spec}(M_t)$ with one fitted $\kappa$ and six fitted threshold levels, on all $\dlat$ with no hold-out. Three things change under \cref{prop:fourbulk-main}. First, the square is not derived: absorbed amplitude $1-e^{-\lambda_is}$, absorbed loss $1-e^{-2\lambda_is}$ and learned energy $(1-e^{-\lambda_is})^2$ are monotone reparametrisations of one another per mode and differ only in how an average weights slow modes (\cref{prop:mode-clocks-pred,rem:square-pred}). Second, $\kappa$ is not $\dlat$-independent: the data-side rate is $a_1(q_d)^2\lambda_i(M_t)/\dlat$ with $q_d=\operatorname{tr}M_t/\dlat$ (\cref{prop:data-side-rates-pred}). Third and decisive, $\mathrm{spec}(M_t)$ labels only the $\dlat$ data-side modes; the $\nsamp-\dlat$ sample modes that carry memorisation are absent from it (\cref{rem:spec-Mt-misses-mem-pred}). Gaussian equivalence resolves them per training point for the noise-averaged $\Umat$ (\cref{prop:sample-bulk-persample-pred}, sketched): with $q_\mu=e^{-2t}\|x^\mu\|^2/\dlat+\Delta_t$ and $\varrho_\mu=1-\Delta_t/q_\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-mu-pred-main}
\Lambda_\mu=\frac{g(q_\mu,\varrho_\mu)}{\nsamp},\qquad g(q,\varrho)=\sum_{k\ \mathrm{odd}\ge3}c_k(q)^2\varrho^k ,
\end{equation}
where $c_k$ are the normalised Hermite coefficients of $\tanh(\sqrt q\,\cdot)$ (\cref{lem:fourbulk-kernel}), so that $g(q,1-\Delta_t/q)=r_t(q)\,a_*(q)^2$, up to a Marchenko--Pastur spread of relative width $\sqrt{\nsamp/\pwidth}$ (at $\dlat=200$ the predicted sample-bulk median, mass and floor mass are within $5\%$, $2\%$ and $8\%$ of the measured values; below $\dlat\approx60$ the mass migrates into $\approx k$ cluster-collective modes, \cref{rem:collective-pred}, conjectured). Because $g$ is increasing in $q$ and $q_d$ falls with $\dlat$, excess dimensions de-saturate the nonlinearity and shrink the reproducible sample-specific feature mass; this depends on $\mathrm{spec}(M_t)$ only through $q$, the same channel through which collapsed VAE coordinates act. A training point is returned when its sample mode is absorbed \emph{and} its empirical-score weight condenses, $\mu\in\mathcal D_{t_c}:=\{\mu:d_{\rm NN}(\mu)^2\ge2\Delta_{t_c}\log\nsamp\}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-source-pred}
\widehat F(s)=\frac1\nsamp\sum_{\mu}\mathbf 1\bigl[\mu\in\mathcal D_{t_c}\bigr]\;\mathbf 1\bigl[g(q_\mu,\varrho_\mu)\,s\ge C\nsamp\bigr],
\end{equation}
with one scalar $C$ (\cref{def:two-source-predictor-pred,cor:shape-from-qmu-pred}): points are memorised in decreasing order of latent norm and the curve's shape in $\log s$ is the distribution of $\log g(q_\mu,\varrho_\mu)$. On the hitting-time tables (\cref{tab:hitting-times-real}) the one-constant clock $\tau\propto\nsamp/g(q_d,\varrho_d)$ reaches leave-one-$\dlat$-out log-errors of $0.41$ (CelebA, $\dlat\ge50$) and $0.15$ (CIFAR-10, $\dlat\ge60$) against $0.34$ and $0.15$ for the seven-parameter in-sample fit, but explains only $1.8\times$ of the $2.1$--$3.9\times$ observed slowdown, and $C$ differs by $2\times$ between datasets at identical optimiser settings (\cref{app:theory-predictor}).

\paragraph{Real latent spectra: effective dimension and posterior collapse (\cref{app:theory-general}).}\label{sec:theory-real}
Real VAE latents are not two-atom: with $\Sigma_0=Z^{\top}Z/\nsamp$ the second moment of the encoder means, $\lambda_i(M_t)=e^{-2t}s_i+\Delta_t$ is a continuous, power-law-like spectrum with an exact atom at the diffusion floor $\Delta_t$. Nothing above needs two atoms: under the Gaussian-equivalence decomposition $\Umat=\Umat^{\rm lin}+\Umat^{\rm res}$ the $\dlat$ largest eigenvalues of $\Umat$ are the pushforward $\lambda\mapsto a_1(q)^2\lambda/\dlat$ of the sampling-broadened spectrum of $M_t$, to relative accuracy $O(\sqrt{\dlat/\pwidth})$ plus a Weyl shift $\le c_+a_*(q)^2/\nsamp$, and the next $\nsamp-\dlat$ form the residual bulk (\cref{prop:pushforward-real}, proved conditional on \cref{assump:gep-real}); a general spectrum replaces the noise-dimension bulk by a data-side continuum plus a floor bulk of $d_{\rm floor}=\#\{i:\lambda_i(M_t)\le(1+\delta)\Delta_t\}$ modes of median $a_1(q)^2\Delta_t/\dlat$. With $d_{\rm eff}=(\sum_is_i)^2/\sum_is_i^2$ (\cref{def:deff-real}) and $\lambda_{(\theta)}$ a variance quantile of the live spectrum, the buffer ratio for a general spectrum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-real-main}
R^{(\theta)}=\frac{\nsamp}{c_+\dlat}\,\frac{a_1(q)^2}{a_*(q)^2}\,\lambda_{(\theta)},\qquad q=\frac{\operatorname{tr}M_t}{\dlat},
\end{equation}
in which no count of coordinates appears. A posterior-collapsed coordinate ($z^\mu_i\equiv0$) sits exactly at $\lambda_i=\Delta_t$, has the exactly linear score $-x_{t,i}/\Delta_t$, and adds exactly $\Delta_t/\dlat$ to $q$ (\cref{lem:dead-real}, proved); appending $n_{\rm dead}$ of them to $d_0$ live ones gives $q=\Delta_t+\tfrac{d_0}{\dlat}(q_0-\Delta_t)$, so $\taugen$ grows at least linearly in $n_{\rm dead}$, $\taumem^{\rm onset}$ grows but by at most $a_*(q_0)^2/a_*(\Delta_t)^2$, and $R\to0$ (\cref{prop:deadblock-real}). On CelebA the measured spectra show exactly this regime for $\dlat\ge160$: $d_{\rm eff}=89.5,90.3,87.9$ at $\dlat=160,180,200$, at least $15\%$ of coordinates within $10^{-4}$ of the floor, and only $q$ moving ($1.47\to1.25$). Treating the RFNN surrogate as a proxy for the trained MLP, the $q$-mechanism predicts hitting times $\times1.21$ and a final fraction $0.56$ at $\dlat=200$ relative to $\dlat=160$, against observed $\times1.26$--$1.41$ and $0.45$; on CIFAR-10 ($160\to260$) it predicts $\taumem\times1.54$ and $0.47$ against $\times1.6$--$2.7$ and $0.125$ (\cref{cor:flatdeff-real}, conjectural). So the mechanism accounts for the sign and for $0.55$--$0.82$ of the log-slowdown of CelebA hitting times, but for only $0.28$ ($0.15$ on CIFAR-10) of the log-decline of the final fraction; the released buffer proxy $\dlat-d_{\rm eff}=71,90,112$ enters no timescale, memorisation is not a function of $q$ alone across datasets, and the remainder lies outside the frozen-feature surrogate (\cref{rem:blunt-real}). We therefore report $d_{\rm eff}$, $d_{\rm floor}$ and $q$ alongside $\dlat$ for every real-data run.

\paragraph{Regime boundaries (\cref{app:theory-scope}).}
The four-bulk picture requires frozen features, the ordering $\dint<\dlat<\nsamp<\pwidth$, a data-dominated null block $\rho_\perp:=e^{-2t}\signoise^2/\beta_t^2\ge\tfrac12$, Gaussian equivalence, and at least a decade between the noise-dimension block and the sample top (\cref{asm:scope-standing}). Three boundaries matter for the sweeps. (a) $\dlat\to\nsamp$: $\psi_n=\nsamp/\dlat$ is not fixed and falls to $2.5$ at $\dlat=200$; the sample block $\nsamp-\dlat$ empties, the noise-dimension lower edge $\propto(1-\sqrt{\gamma_\perp})^2\to0$ closes onto the sample top, and the converged null score $-\hat\Sigma_{t,\perp}^{-1}$ acquires sample-specificity $\varepsilon_\perp^2=\rho_\perp^2\gamma_\perp(1+O(\rho_\perp\gamma_\perp))$ on the fast clock $\dlat/(a_1^2\beta_t^2)$ (\cref{prop:scope-nullspec}, proved). Memorisation empirically \emph{returns} at large $\dlat$, but only in the width-scaled MLP sweep (hidden $8\dlat$: fractions $0,0.002,0.014$ at $\dlat=100,150,200$, $\signoise=0.5$; $0$ at $\signoise=0.01$) and not at fixed width; we conjecture that $\varepsilon_\perp$ is the necessary ingredient and capacity the second, which predicts $\dlat^{\rm reb}$ linear in $\nsamp$ (\cref{rem:scope-rebound-conj}); a linear null score alone cannot produce NN-ratio memorisation. (b) $\signoise\to0$: the noise-dimension block sits on the $W$-broadened diffusion floor $a_1^2\Delta_t(1\pm\psi_p^{-1/2})^2/\dlat$ and the decade separation requires $\signoise\ge\signoise^*$, with $\signoise^*=0.91$ at $\dlat=10$ falling to $0.40$--$0.50$ for $\dlat\ge40$ (\cref{prop:scope-sigperp}): the $\signoise=0.01$ sweeps are a three-bulk regime in which counts and $\taugen$ apply but the buffer separation and the rebound do not; the fixed-null-energy control $\signoise^2=c/(\dlat-\dint)$ removes $\rho_\perp$ while keeping the count and the $q$-dilution, so the frozen-feature theory predicts that the delay survives it and the four-bulk structure does not (\cref{prop:scope-control}). (c) $\pwidth=\dlat+\nsamp+300$ violates $\psi_p>1+\psi_n$ uniformly: block \emph{means} are $\pwidth$-independent but widths broaden by $(1\pm\sqrt{(\dlat-\dint)/\pwidth})^2$, a factor $6.8$ at $\dlat=200$, and the decade separation is predicted to fail for $\dlat\gtrsim100$ (\cref{rem:scope-psip}). Also, $\dint$ fixed makes the ``signal bulk'' a finite-rank spike set throughout, and the synthetic score-error curves of earlier drafts were computed with a transposed whitening and constrain nothing here.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Everything above is a frozen-feature, fixed-$t$, gradient-flow statement about clustered Gaussian data on a linear subspace. Not covered: trainable first layers (the one-step conjecture of \cref{rem:scope-feature} is the only statement, and it predicts a \emph{shorter} absolute buffer than the frozen value), curved manifolds and non-Gaussian cluster shapes, adaptive optimisers (the MLP sweeps use Adam and carry no clock statement), the multi-$t$ training of the real-data models, the decoder's response to imperfectly learned floor coordinates, and DiT-scale architectures; \cref{rem:scope-outside} lists every configuration in the paper that falls outside the hypotheses.
