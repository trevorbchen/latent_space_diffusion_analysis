\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What is established.}
At fixed hidden width, widening the latent space delays memorization: the synthetic memorized fraction falls from $30\%$ to near zero over $\dlat=5\to40$, the decline survives the signal-projected test (\cref{tab:e2-projection}), and post-identity-threshold comparisons give $3.6\times$ and $1.4\times$ increases in the mean $5\%$ hitting time on CelebA and CIFAR-10. In the frozen-feature model the measured spectral separation grows as $q$ falls, and the fixed-$q$ control (\cref{rem:normfixed-buf}) supports an operating-point explanation rather than a mode-count explanation.

\paragraph{The causal gap.}
Whether this mechanism operates in trained networks is the main open question. In the synthetic MLPs the first-layer pre-activation variance is the same at every width at initialization and at 50k steps ($q(40)/q(5)=0.96$ and $1.00$); it separates only later, growing together with memorization at small $\dlat$ (ratio $0.31$ at 5M steps). These observations do not establish that a width-set operating point causes the MLP delay. On real data the surrogate only partly captures the slowdown, and its fitted clock does not transfer across datasets with a single constant.

\paragraph{Boundaries.}
The spatial-DiT pilot on CelebA, with a $4\times4\times\dlat$ latent grid, does not show the decline: memorization stays high across channel counts (\cref{fig:app-spatial-dit-summary}), so the effect is established only for vector latents with MLP denoisers. All sweeps use $\nsamp\le1000$ training points, and a sweep that scales width with $\dlat$ is a capacity sweep. What would close the gap is a trained-network quantity that is set by width before memorization begins and predicts its onset; a candidate consistent with the data is the per-coordinate gap between the empirical and population scores, which grows $3.6\times$ from $\dlat=5$ to $40$.
